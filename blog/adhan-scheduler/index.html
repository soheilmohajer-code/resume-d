Building a Resilient Adhan Scheduler on Android

AlarmManager lessons from shipping Gah-e Saba: exact alarms, doze mode, OEM battery killers, boot receivers — and how to make scheduling code you cannot fully control testable.

DATE: Oct 18, 2026READ: ~2 MINAUTHOR: SOHEIL MOHAJER
$ ./gradlew assembleReleaseBUILD: SUCCESSFUL[OK]

Gah-e Saba shows prayer times for the user's selected city and fires an adhan notification at the exact minute. On my emulator, that was fifteen minutes of work. On real devices it became a two-month education in everything Android allows you to think you scheduled. This post collects the lessons that survived contact with production.

The problem with "exact" alarms

setExactAndAllowWhileIdle() promises exact delivery, but the contract is softer than the name suggests. From Android 12 the user must explicitly grant the exact-alarm permission, and the system may still defer under heavy load. The practical pattern is to schedule with a fallback chain:

AdhanScheduler.kt
fun scheduleNext(context: Context, at: LocalDateTime) {
    val trigger = at.toEpochMillis(ZONE)
    val pi = pendingIntent(context, REQUEST_NEXT)

    // exact when permitted, inexact otherwise — never skip
    if (canScheduleExactAlarms(context)) {
        alarmManager.setExactAndAllowWhileIdle(RTC, trigger, pi)
    } else {
        alarmManager.setWindow(RTC, trigger, 120_000, pi)
    }
}

An alarm that is one minute late is a product bug. An alarm that never fires is an uninstalled app.

Doze, battery optimization and OEM killers

Stock Android is the easy case. Xiaomi, Huawei and Samsung ship their own "battery savers" that happily kill your process and cancel alarms with no API surface to detect it. Three mitigations that measurably worked:

  • Ask for battery-optimization exemption — with an honest explanation, not a dark pattern.
  • Re-verify the alarm on every app open; re-schedule from the foreground when possible.
  • Use a WorkManager periodic job purely as a repair mechanism, not as the scheduler.

Users who granted "unrestricted background" on Xiaomi devices went from ~60% missed alarms to effectively zero in my crash-free analytics. Document the exact tap path per OEM in your FAQ.

Re-scheduling: boot, timezone, date change

Alarms die on reboot, and prayer times shift every day. The receiver graph ended up small but dense:

AndroidManifest.xml
<receiver android:name=".boot.BootReceiver" android:exported="false">
    <intent-filter>
        <action android:name="android.intent.action.BOOT_COMPLETED"/>
        <action android:name="android.intent.action.TIMEZONE_CHANGED"/>
        <action android:name="android.intent.action.TIME_SET"/>
        <action android:name="android.intent.action.DATE_CHANGED"/>
    </intent-filter>
</receiver>

Every path converges on one idempotent rescheduleEverything(): cancel by fixed request codes, recompute today's times, schedule the next one. Idempotency matters more than cleverness — TIME_SET can fire several times per minute while the user drags the time picker.

Event Frequency Recompute
BOOT_COMPLETED rare full
TIMEZONE_CHANGED rare full + city check
TIME_SET bursty debounced
alarm fired 5x/day next only

Making the scheduler testable

The breakthrough was refusing to let AlarmManager appear anywhere near business logic. The app computes a plain list of ScheduledMoment(time, type); a thin adapter maps that list onto platform calls. Unit tests assert on the list; one instrumented test covers the adapter.

TL;DR: trust nothing, re-verify everything, keep one idempotent repair path, and test the plan — not the platform.
