notification at the exact minute. On my emulator, that was fifteen minutes of
work. On real devices it became a two-month education in everything Android
allows you to <em>think</em> you scheduled. This post collects the lessons that
survived contact with production.</p>

load. The practical pattern is to schedule with a fallback chain:</p>

<h2 id="doze-battery-optimization-and-oem-killers">Doze, battery optimization and OEM killers</h2><p>Stock Android is the easy case. Xiaomi, Huawei and Samsung ship their own
&quot;battery savers&quot; that happily kill your process and cancel alarms with no API
surface to detect it. Three mitigations that measurably worked:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ask for battery-optimization exemption — with an honest explanation, not a dark pattern.</li>
<li>Re-verify the alarm on every app open; re-schedule from the foreground when possible.</li>
<li>Use a <code>WorkManager</code> periodic job purely as a <em>repair</em> mechanism, not as the scheduler.</li>
</ul>
<p>Users who granted &quot;unrestricted background&quot; on Xiaomi devices went from ~60%
missed alarms to effectively zero in my crash-free analytics. Document the
exact tap path per OEM in your FAQ.</p>

<h2 id="making-the-scheduler-testable">Making the scheduler testable</h2><p>The breakthrough was refusing to let <code>AlarmManager</code> appear anywhere near
business logic. The app computes a plain list of <code>ScheduledMoment(time, type)</code>; a thin adapter maps that list onto platform calls. Unit tests assert
on the list; one instrumented test covers the adapter.</p>
<p><strong>TL;DR:</strong> trust nothing, re-verify everything, keep one idempotent repair
path, and test the plan — not the platform.</p>
